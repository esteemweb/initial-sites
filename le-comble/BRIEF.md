# LE COMBLE — full brief

A restaurant with rooms, in Croix-Rousse, Lyon.
Everything needed to build the brand and the site, in one document.

---

## 1. The premise

**This is a restaurant that happens to have nineteen rooms above it. Not a hotel with a dining room.**

That distinction decides everything. The chef is the story, not the concierge. Half the people who eat here never stay. The restaurant is open to the city six nights a week, and a guest upstairs is booking a table first and a bed second.

Most hotel sites are written for people arriving from an airport. This one is written for someone in Lyon deciding where to eat on Thursday — and, separately, for someone in London deciding where to stay in October.

---

## 2. The name

**Le Comble** — the attic, the top floor under the roof beams. It is also the word for the height of something: *le comble du luxe*, the height of luxury.

The building rises four storeys up a hill that already sits above the city. The name says where it is and what it aims at, in one word, without explaining itself.

It is also a small joke. *C'est le comble* means "that's the last straw" — a bit of Lyonnais dryness the brand can live with.

---

## 3. The building

A silk workshop on rue Burdeau, Croix-Rousse, built 1831.

Croix-Rousse is the hill where Lyon's silk industry worked. The buildings were put up for looms, not for people — which is why they have **four-metre ceilings and windows nearly two metres tall.** The Jacquard loom was enormous and needed height and daylight. Those proportions are the reason the rooms feel the way they do, and they cannot be faked in a new build.

The ground floor was the workshop. It's now the dining room, with the original beams and the floor it always had.

A *traboule* — one of Lyon's covered passages cutting through buildings between streets — runs along the side of the building. Guests use it to reach the courtyard. It's the kind of detail that exists nowhere else in the world.

---

## 4. The chef

**Camille Ferrand**, 34, from Villeurbanne.

Trained in Lyon bouchons from sixteen, then six years in Copenhagen. Came back in 2022 because she was tired of cooking food with no grandmother behind it.

Her position, in her own words: *Lyon cooks better than anywhere in France and eats heavier than anywhere in France. Those are two different facts and people keep treating them as one.*

The food is Lyonnaise technique with a lighter hand. The quenelle is still a quenelle. There is just less cream in it.

---

## 5. The restaurant — Navette

Named after the weaver's shuttle, which crosses back and forth all day.

- 38 covers in the dining room, 16 in the courtyard from May to September
- Dinner Tuesday to Saturday, lunch Friday and Saturday
- One menu at €68, five courses, no à la carte. It changes every three weeks.
- A short carte at the bar for people who want one plate and a glass
- The wine list is Rhône-heavy and there is a lot of it by the glass

**Non-residents are the majority of the room.** This is stated openly on the site, because in Lyon a hotel restaurant is assumed to be for tourists and this one isn't.

---

## 6. The bar — Le Toit

The roof. Fourteen seats, first come first served, no bookings.

Open from six. It exists because Croix-Rousse sits above the city and the view down over the two rivers is the best thing the building owns.

---

## 7. The rooms

Nineteen rooms across three floors, named for what the floors were.

| Type | Count | What it is | From |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mansarde** | 7 | Under the roof. Smaller, beamed, angled ceilings. The cheapest and the most atmospheric. | €190 |
| **Trame** | 9 | The middle floors. Four-metre ceilings, tall shuttered windows, most with a view over the rooftops. | €260 |
| **Atelier** | 2 | The old workshop floor. Double-height, the full bank of original windows, north light all day. | €380 |
| **Grand Atelier** | 1 | The corner workshop. Two aspects, a freestanding bath under the window, the traboule entrance. | €520 |

Breakfast is €18 and it's served in the restaurant. Nobody eats it in bed here; there are no trays.

**No children under 10.** Stated plainly, with the reason: the dining room is small and the building carries sound.

## 8. Private hire

The dining room privatises for 40 seated, 70 standing. The whole building — nineteen rooms and the restaurant — goes for weddings and company gatherings, usually Sunday to Tuesday when the restaurant is closed anyway.

This is an enquiry, not a booking. A form that asks the right questions and a promise of a reply within two working days.

---

## 9. Who comes

**For the restaurant:** Lyonnais, 30–60, who eat out seriously. They are not impressed by the fact that there are rooms upstairs.

**For the rooms:** Europeans on a two- or three-night trip, usually a couple, often coming specifically for food. Lyon is a two-hour train from Paris and two from Marseille, so most arrive by train, not car. The site should know that.

**For private hire:** Lyon companies, and families doing a wedding without a château.

---

## 10. What this site must do

**Three booking paths from one control**, the way the reference site handles rooms, tables and spa:

1. **A room** — dates, guests, room type, availability, seasonal rates, three-step confirmation with no payment
2. **A table** — date, service, party size, live availability by sitting, held for the guest's name
3. **The building** — a private hire enquiry form

**Two languages, French and English**, with the French as the primary. A Lyon restaurant whose site is English-first is a tourist restaurant, and this one isn't.

**Pages:** home, the restaurant, the menu, the bar, rooms, room detail, the building and its history, private hire, Camille, getting here, FAQ, contact, 404.

## 11. Visual identity

**Take the architecture, not the postcard.**

**Colour:** off-white plaster, and a deep indigo — the colour silk dye vats ran in this quarter. One warm brass for small accents. No Provençal blues, no lavender, no sun-bleached Mediterranean palette. This is a northern French industrial hill town, not the Côte d'Azur.

**Type:** a high-contrast serif for headings, because Lyon is a printing city as much as a silk one. A quiet grotesque for everything else. No script fonts, ever.

**The system:** the grid comes from the loom. Warp and weft — strict vertical columns crossed by horizontal rules. Very tall image crops, mirroring the windows. Where the reference site pairs two photographs per section, do the same, but portrait rather than landscape.

**Photography:** the room proportions, not the bedding. Daylight through tall windows, the beams, the traboule, hands working in the kitchen, the food shot from above on plain plates. No models in robes. No aerial drone shots. No candles.

---

## 12. Voice

Direct, unsentimental, a little proud. French pragmatism, not hospitality warmth.

> The dining room seats 38. Most nights it's full. Book ahead or eat at the bar.

> We are on a hill. It is steep. There is a funicular and we will tell you which one.

> There is no spa, no gym and no pool. There is a restaurant, and it is the reason to come.

Never: *nestled*, *sanctuary*, *oasis*, *your home away from home*, *culinary journey*, *passionate*.

---

## 13. What to take from the reference site, and what to leave

**Take:**
- The single "Book" control that splits into three paths
- Room types named as worlds rather than as grades
- Paired images per section
- An FAQ that answers the awkward question honestly — for them it's "the hotel isn't actually in Saint-Tropez." For us it's "yes, the hill is steep," and "no, you don't need to be staying here to eat here."
- Non-residents made welcome explicitly
- A clear age policy stated with its reason

**Leave:**
- The full-screen background video. It costs weight and says nothing.
- The offer pop-up.
- Fourteen footer links. Ours has six.
- The wellness vocabulary — *lâcher prise*, *se reconnecter à l'essentiel*. Le Comble would never.
- Saint-Tropez luxury signalling generally. This place is €190 a night on a hill, not €399 for a day pass.

---

## 14. The one rule

Every decision answers to the same question: **would the chef say this out loud?**

If it sounds like a hotel brochure, it's wrong.
